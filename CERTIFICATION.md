# LightBurn Protocol Certification Framework

As the LightBurn Protocol ecosystem grows, we may introduce optional compatibility or certification programs intended to help users identify implementations that meet certain interoperability, stability, and usability expectations.
This document exists primarily to explain the general direction and intent of such programs should they be introduced in the future.

## Purpose

The goal of certification would not be to restrict ecosystem participation or prevent experimentation.
Instead, the goal would be to reduce confusion for end users and help distinguish between implementations that have undergone additional validation and those that have not.
A healthy open ecosystem benefits from both experimentation and predictability, and certification is intended to support the latter where appropriate.

## Community Implementations

Anyone is free to implement the protocol under the terms of the MIT License.
Many implementations may remain entirely community-driven, unofficial, experimental, or specialized, and that is perfectly acceptable.
Community implementations should avoid presenting themselves in ways that imply official approval or certification unless such approval has actually been granted.

## Certified Implementations
If formal certification programs are introduced, they may involve areas such as:
*   protocol compliance
*   interoperability testing
*   version compatibility
*   error handling behavior
*   user experience expectations
*   implementation consistency
*   stability testing
*   support expectations

Requirements may vary depending on the type of product or implementation involved.
Certification may also be version-specific rather than permanent.

## Safety and Regulatory Responsibility
Even if a product or implementation were to participate in a future certification process, responsibility for machine safety, electrical compliance, fire prevention, and regulatory obligations would remain entirely with the manufacturer or implementer.
Certification should not be interpreted as a regulatory approval, safety guarantee, or legal compliance review.

## Revocation
If an implementation is found to be materially incompatible, misleading, unsafe, or harmful to users or the ecosystem, any certification or compatibility status may be suspended or revoked.

## Future Evolution
The ecosystem will evolve over time, and any future certification framework would likely evolve with it.
Requirements, testing methodologies, compatibility expectations, and participation models may change as implementation experience grows and the needs of the ecosystem become clearer.
The intent is not to create unnecessary barriers, but to help maintain trust, interoperability, and a reasonable user experience across the broader ecosystem.
