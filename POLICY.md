# LightBurn Protocol Usage and Branding Policy
The LightBurn Protocol is released under the MIT License to encourage broad adoption and interoperability across the ecosystem. At the same time, we want to make sure users are not misled about what is officially supported, reviewed, or certified by LightBurn or MillMage Software.
This document explains how we approach branding, compatibility claims, certification, and general ecosystem participation. It does not replace or modify the rights granted under the MIT License.

## Compatibility Claims
We do not object to developers or manufacturers truthfully stating that their products implement or support the LightBurn Protocol.
Statements such as “Compatible with the LightBurn Protocol” or “Supports the LightBurn Protocol” are generally acceptable provided they are accurate and not misleading.
However, products should not be presented in a way that suggests official endorsement, partnership, certification, or approval unless such a relationship has actually been established with us in writing.

## Trademarks and Branding
LightBurn, MillMage, DigiFable, associated logos, and related branding remain the property of MillMage Software.
The MIT License grants permission to use the protocol and related source materials, but it does not grant rights to use our trademarks or branding in ways that could confuse users or imply an official relationship.
In practical terms, this means you should avoid presenting products as “official,” “certified,” or “approved” unless they have actually gone through such a process.

## Safety Responsibilities
Because the protocol may be used with lasers, CNC systems, and other hardware capable of causing physical harm or property damage, implementers are responsible for ensuring their own systems behave safely and comply with applicable regulations.
That responsibility includes things like motion safety, thermal protections, interlocks, emergency stop systems, electrical compliance, and general operational safety.
Publishing or implementing the protocol should not be interpreted as a safety review or certification by LightBurn or MillMage Software.

## Support Expectations
We are enthusiastic about broad participation, but we are not able to guarantee support for every third-party implementation or modified protocol variant.
In some situations, we may ask users to reproduce issues using officially supported hardware, firmware, or software before we can investigate further.
Manufacturers and developers building commercial implementations are generally expected to provide first-line support for their own products.

## Future Certification Programs
As the ecosystem matures, we may introduce optional compatibility or certification programs intended to help users identify implementations that meet certain interoperability, stability, or usability expectations.
Any such programs would be separate from the MIT License itself and would likely involve additional testing or validation requirements.

## Protocol Evolution
The protocol will continue evolving over time. Features may be added, clarified, revised, or deprecated as implementation experience grows.
Developers are encouraged to version carefully, test thoroughly, and avoid relying on undocumented behavior.

## Final Notes
Our goal with the protocol is to encourage an open and healthy ecosystem while still protecting users from confusion around branding, compatibility, and safety.
We want developers and manufacturers to build on top of the protocol successfully, and we hope these guidelines help establish reasonable expectations for everyone involved.
